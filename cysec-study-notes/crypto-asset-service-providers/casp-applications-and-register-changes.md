# What did a CASP registration application involve, and how were changes reported?

What a CASP had to submit to join CySEC's register, what CySEC checked and how long it had, and the deadlines for reporting changes afterwards. · By the [ExamPass CY editorial team](../about.md) · Last reviewed 2 October 2026 · 5 min read

Tested in: AML · Ch 8

<a id="short-answer"></a>

## Short answer

A CASP applied to CySEC with its register details plus **all the public crypto-asset addresses, public keys or digital wallets it controlled**, the crypto-assets it dealt in, its client types, any crypto ATMs and the jurisdictions it served. CySEC checked that its managers and beneficiaries were **honest and competent** and that its AML and safeguarding procedures were sound, and had **six months** to decide. Afterwards, changes to identity details needed an amendment request **within one month**, other changes a notice **within 15 days**, and material changes **prior approval**.

<a id="key-facts"></a>

## Applications and changes at a glance

| Point | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Crypto-asset addresses | All public addresses of crypto-assets and/or public keys or digital wallets the CASP controls |
| Fit and proper | Managers: a good reputation, the necessary knowledge, skills and experience, and enough time. Beneficiaries: good reputation and the ability to keep the CASP financially sound |
| CySEC's decision | Within six months of a duly completed application |
| Name, trade name, legal form, LEI or address | Request to amend the registration within one month of the change; CySEC updates the register within three days |
| Crypto-assets, client types, payment services, ATMs, jurisdictions, other registrations | Notify CySEC within 15 days of the change |
| Services, crypto-asset addresses, Board or senior management, beneficiaries, website | Material change: CySEC's approval needed beforehand |
| Today | No new national applications since 17 October 2024; MiCA authorisation instead, with completeness checked within 25 working days and a decision within 40 working days of a complete application (a pause of up to 20 working days is possible) |

Source: CySEC Directive R.A.D. 269/2021, as amended to R.A.D. 343/2023; Law 188(I)/2007, Article 61E; Regulation (EU) 2023/1114 (MiCA), Articles 62 and 63.

## In the exam

The exam is written from the exam material, which does not reflect the changes below. Expect its answer. If that answer is not among the options and the current rule is, choose the current rule.

- **Applications and decision time**

  **Exam material:** A CASP applies to CySEC for entry in the register, and CySEC decides within six months of a duly completed application.

  **Current law (since 17 October 2024 (CySEC announcement ending national applications); 30 December 2024 for MiCA authorisation (Regulation (EU) 2023/1114)):** CySEC no longer accepts national applications. Under MiCA, an application is checked for completeness within 25 working days and decided within 40 working days of being complete.

<a id="application"></a>

## What did a CASP have to submit, and what did CySEC check?

A duly completed application had to contain the details that would appear in the public register (name, trade name, legal form, LEI, physical address, services and website) and more. The CASP listed its crypto-asset addresses, meaning every public address of crypto-assets and every public key or digital wallet under its control, not postal or email addresses. It also stated which crypto-assets it dealt in, the types of clients it served, whether it offered payment services in crypto-assets, whether it ran crypto-asset ATMs and, if so, how many and where, the jurisdictions it operated in, and whether it was registered or supervised anywhere else.

CySEC first checked that the application was complete and then whether the applicant met the conditions for registration. The people holding management positions had to be honest and competent: of good reputation, with the knowledge, skills and experience needed, and able to devote enough time to their duties. The beneficiaries had to be of good reputation and able to keep the CASP financially sound. CySEC also looked at the applicant's close links with other persons, its AML/CFT policies and procedures, the procedures for running the business properly, and its controls to minimise the risk of clients' crypto-assets being stolen or lost. CySEC had six months to assess the application and tell the applicant whether it would be registered.

Terms used in this note

- **Crypto-asset address** — A public address, public key or digital wallet used to hold or transfer crypto-assets on a blockchain.

- **Beneficiary** — In the CASP rules, a person who, alone or with others, directly or indirectly holds a qualifying holding in the CASP (at least 10% of the capital or voting rights, or significant influence).

- **Material change** — A change to a registered CASP's services, crypto-asset addresses, Board or senior management, beneficiaries or website, which needed CySEC's prior approval.

<a id="changes"></a>

## How were changes to the registered information reported?

Changes were sorted by type. A change to the CASP's name, trade name, legal form, LEI or physical address required a request to amend the registration within one month of the change, and CySEC updated the register within three days of receiving it.

A change to operational information, such as the crypto-assets used, the types of clients, crypto-asset payment services, crypto-asset ATMs, the jurisdictions of operation, or registration or supervision elsewhere, had to be notified to CySEC within 15 days of the change at the latest.

Changes to the services or activities, the crypto-asset addresses, the Board or senior management, the beneficiaries or the website were material changes. They needed CySEC's approval before they took effect, as described in [Who had to register as a CASP?](casp-registration-and-mica.md)

<a id="today"></a>

## How does a firm get into the market today?

CySEC stopped accepting national applications on 17 October 2024. A firm that wants to provide crypto-asset services now applies for authorisation under MiCA. The competent authority checks whether the application is complete within 25 working days and decides within 40 working days of receiving a complete application, a period that can be paused for up to 20 working days while it asks for more information. MiCA sets its own requirements on the management body, shareholders, capital and procedures.

<a id="how-to-think"></a>

## How to think about it

Match the change to its clock. Identity details (name, trade name, legal form, LEI, address): one month to ask, three days for CySEC to update. Operational details (crypto-assets, client types, payment services, ATMs, jurisdictions): 15 days. Anything touching services, crypto-asset addresses, the Board or senior management, beneficiaries or the website: approval first. For applications, remember six months for CySEC and that crypto-asset addresses mean blockchain addresses, keys and wallets.

<a id="common-mistakes"></a>

## Common mistakes

1. **Reading crypto-asset addresses as postal or email addresses.** They are the public blockchain addresses, public keys and digital wallets the CASP controls.
2. **Applying the 15-day deadline to a change of name or address.** Identity details need an amendment request within one month; 15 days is for operational information.
3. **Notifying a new Board member after the event.** Changes to the Board or senior management were material changes needing prior approval.
4. **Giving CySEC three months to decide an application.** CySEC had six months from a duly completed application.
